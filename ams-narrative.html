Should AMS Bring Bracket Machining In-House for the Q3 Pilot Line?

A capital-investment decision, written end-to-end in Amazon's own internal 6-page narrative format.

1Context and Strategic Rationale

AMS currently outsources bracket-class machined parts for the Q3 pilot line to a single external tier-1 supplier at a quoted $186/unit across an expected 1,800-unit annual run. Lead time from PO to delivery is 6 weeks, and the supplier has twice slipped delivery by 2+ weeks against a committed date, each time absorbed as pilot-line schedule risk rather than passed back as a penalty. AMS's own Bellevue facility has open 5-axis CNC capacity two shifts a week that is otherwise idle.

The question this document answers: does bringing this part family in-house reduce unit cost, and does it reduce schedule risk enough to justify the capital, labor, and management attention an internal transition requires? This is not a general in-sourcing philosophy question — it is a should-cost-driven answer for this one part family, at this volume, on this timeline.

2Operating Tenets

  • A should-cost model beats a supplier quote as a decision input. The external quote tells us what the supplier will accept; it does not tell us what the part should cost to make. We decide against the second number, and only compare it to the first at the end.
  • Idle capacity is not free capacity. "We already have the machine" is not a should-cost input by itself — the machine's standing cost is sunk either way, but the decision must still clear on running cost, labor, and opportunity cost of the shift capacity it consumes.
  • Schedule risk gets a dollar value, not an adjective. "Risky" is not a line item. The two historical slips are converted into an expected cost of delay before they enter the recommendation.
  • The model is reviewed as code, not defended as an opinion. Every input below is a named assumption with a source or an explicit "estimate" flag — nothing is presented as more certain than it is.

5Strategic Evaluation and Build-vs-Buy Analysis

At 1,800 units/year, the should-cost gap ($85.37/unit) implies ~$154K/year in potential cost avoidance before ramp costs. Against a capital ask of $0 (existing idle capacity — no new machine purchase required for this volume) and a one-time ramp/qualification cost estimated at $38K (first-article inspection, fixture build, 5 weeks of efficiency-negative labor per Section 4), payback is inside the first year on the cost line alone.

Schedule-risk value: two historical 2-week slips against a committed pilot-line date, each estimated (program-management input, not a should-cost figure) at $40-60K in downstream schedule impact. Bringing the part in-house does not guarantee zero slips, but removes the single-source, 6-week-lead-time dependency entirely — treated here as risk reduction, not a hard dollar credit, to avoid double-counting a soft benefit as if it were should-cost.

Sensitivity: even if the should-cost model understates true cost by 20% (a stress case for setup-time and scrap assumptions), the in-house cost remains $120.76/unit — still $65/unit below the external quote. The recommendation is not sensitive to being modestly wrong.

6Implementation Plan, Milestones, and Risk Mitigation

Wk 1-2First-article qualification run (10 units) against the existing supplier's print — no schedule commitment made against this batch.
Wk 3-4Fixture and standard-work finalization; labor efficiency variance tracked daily against the Section 4 ramp assumption, not averaged monthly.
Wk 5-8Parallel run — 50% volume in-house, 50% external — before fully exiting the supplier PO, so a should-cost miss is caught at half exposure, not full.
Risk: ramp efficiency worse than modeledTrigger: labor efficiency variance exceeds 15% unfavorable past week 6. Action: extend the parallel-run window rather than force the exit date.
Risk: should-cost model missing a real cost driverTrigger: actual-to-should-cost variance exceeds the 20% stress case in Section 5 for two consecutive months post-ramp. Action: full model re-audit before committing further part families to the same in-source pattern.

Recommendation: proceed with qualification (Weeks 1-4) under the parallel-run structure above. Full supplier exit is a separate, later decision gated on the Week 5-8 data, not pre-committed here.
