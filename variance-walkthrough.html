A Real Labor Variance, Traced to Root Cause

B.E. Meyers & Co. — Redmond, WA — Feb–Jun 2026. Not a hypothetical: this is the same $208K labor-variance trace stated on my resume, walked through step by step.

What this is — and isn't yet. Every stage below is grounded in what's already public on my resume. Two stages (marked) are honestly thinner than a full case study should be, because the resume states the outcome, not every operational detail — I'd rather show the real shape of the investigation than pad it with invented specifics that sound good but aren't true. Ask me about the missing detail directly; I'd rather say it live than fake it here.
1

Symptom

A ~$208,000 unexplained labor variance surfaced in the manufacturing P&L at B.E. Meyers — an ITAR-regulated defense manufacturer where I was the first dedicated cost accountant in company history. No prior cost-accounting function, no BI team, no documented SYSPRO schema to start from.
2

Why it couldn't just be "explained away"

With no predecessor and no existing variance-reporting process, there was no template answer to reach for — "explain a $208K variance" meant building the traceability path from scratch, not looking it up in an existing report.
3

Method

Mapped the SYSPRO 8 SQL schema across purchasing, receiving, WIP, and inventory myself — joins that had never been formally documented at the company — then traced the variance to its shop-floor root cause via a direct SQL query against that schema, rather than working from the ERP's own summary reports.
4

Root cause

Traced to a specific shop-floor root cause — this is where the resume states the outcome (traced and fixed) without naming the mechanism.

5

Fix

Built real-time tooling so the same variance stopped recurring — the resume's own language, describing a preventive control, not a one-time reconciliation.

6

Outcome

Documented variance narratives for leadership, reviewed by the CFO — replacing single-point cost estimates with CFO-reviewed, market-reflective baselines as the standing practice going forward, not a one-off fire drill.
