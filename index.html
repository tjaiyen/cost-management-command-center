Who's actually watching the contingency reserve?

A cost-management methodology demo built around a synthetic capital-development program — 7 tabs, real math computed live in this page's own JavaScript, no build step, no server, no dependency to install.

The program is synthetic — no employer, client, or agency data appears anywhere in this repository. Real facts are threaded through it and badge-labeled inline wherever they appear: a real $208K variance root-cause trace, a real 100+ bid-package contingency-tracking pattern, AACE International's real Recommended Practices (RP 57R-09, 65R-11, 44R-08, 17R-97, 40R-08), and real published data-center cost-driver figures (Turner & Townsend's cost index; electrical/cooling cost-split percentages). Everything else — the program name, dollar amounts, dates, vendor labels — is invented to make the methodology legible and marked illustrative.

Contingency Drawdown & AACE RP 57R-09 Monte Carlo Band real method

Assigns Beta-PERT/Triangular/Log-Normal probability distributions (optimistic/most-likely/pessimistic bounds) to WBS cost and duration line items, then runs many simulated draws to produce a cumulative distribution curve — P50 (median), P80, P90 confidence thresholds for sizing contingency reserves, instead of a single-point estimate that hides the real range of outcomes.

An elevated drawdown ratio early in a project is itself the early-warning signal. The P50/P80/P90 band follows AACE International RP 57R-09 — a real, named industry practice.

Real vs. Illustrative — the audit-trail discipline itself

This entire build follows one rule: a figure is only unbadged as real if it traces to a cited public source or a real prior-role headline number. Everything else — row-level detail invented to make a chart legible — carries illustrative. This page has been through two independent adversarial stress-test rounds checking that rule is actually followed, not just stated.
