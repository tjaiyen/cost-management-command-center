Who's actually watching the contingency reserve?

A cost-management methodology demo built around a synthetic capital-development program — 11 tabs, real math computed live in this page's own JavaScript, no build step, no server, no dependency to install.

The program is synthetic — no employer, client, or agency data appears anywhere in this repository. Real facts are threaded through it and badge-labeled inline wherever they appear: a real $208K variance root-cause trace, a real 100+ bid-package contingency-tracking pattern, AACE International's real Recommended Practices (RP 57R-09, 65R-11, 44R-08, 17R-97, 40R-08, 58R-10, 68R-11, 86R-14), Turner & Townsend's real Data Centre Construction Cost Index 2025-2026 (market benchmarks in the Cost tab, cited again in the Reference glossary — not a floating claim), and RICS/ICMS & Uptime Institute's real industry standards (see the Reference tab). Everything else — the program name, dollar amounts, dates, vendor labels — is invented to make the methodology legible and marked illustrative. The currency selector converts this program's own illustrative dollar figures only — cited real industry benchmarks (the Cost tab's cost-driver percentages, the Reference tab's glossary) stay in their originally published currency, since converting a cited external figure at an invented exchange rate would misrepresent the source.

Contingency Drawdown & AACE RP 57R-09 Monte Carlo Band real method

Assigns Beta-PERT/Triangular/Log-Normal probability distributions (optimistic/most-likely/pessimistic bounds) to WBS cost and duration line items, then runs many simulated draws to produce a cumulative distribution curve — P50 (median), P80, P90 confidence thresholds for sizing contingency reserves, instead of a single-point estimate that hides the real range of outcomes.

An elevated drawdown ratio early in a project is itself the early-warning signal. The P50/P80/P90 band follows AACE International RP 57R-09 — a real, named industry practice.
